Vocabularies¶

Some columns in the spreadsheets, and some fields in JSON, take a word from a fixed list rather than free text. This page lists every such word, what it means, and the full identifier it stands for in PLATO. It is generated from the ontology, so it always matches it.

In the spreadsheets, write the word in the Value column exactly as shown, with the same capital letters. Four columns are exceptions, because the table definitions add the start of the word for you:

  • attribution: write Stated or Inferred (not AttributionStated);

  • stance: write Reported, Tentative, Doubted or Asserted (not StanceReported);

  • transcription_accuracy: write Accurate, Inaccurate or False;

  • transcription_completeness: write Complete, Reconstructable or NonReconstructable.

The kinds of spatial entity (TypeRoute and the others) are not written as words in any column: give the full identifier in a type row’s type_uri, as Routes, journeys and networks shows. In JSON and RDF, use the full identifier.

The lists are open: if you need a value that is not here, ask for it by opening an issue.

Attribution status¶

Starter concepts for plato:attribution_status. Whether the attribution of evidence to a cited source was stated or resolved editorially.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

AttributionInferred

The source was given as ibidem or idem and resolved by an editor, so the attribution is inferred rather than stated.

https://w3id.org/plato#AttributionInferred

AttributionStated

The source is named where the form occurs. The default attribution status, usually omitted.

https://w3id.org/plato#AttributionStated

Kind of spatial entity¶

Starter concepts for the kinds of SpatialEntity a platform treats specially: routes, itineraries, networks and segments. A Type attestation names one in its type identifier.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

TypeItinerary

A journey made through places in order, each stop with its own timespan: a traveller’s tour, a royal progress, a ship’s voyage. Not plato:Itinerary, which is a geometry role (a line tracing a route or journey).

https://w3id.org/plato#TypeItinerary

TypeNetwork

A set of places and the connections between them, in no single order: a river system, a canal network, a correspondence network.

https://w3id.org/plato#TypeNetwork

TypeRoute

An ordered set of places making a way: a road, a sea lane, a pilgrimage route. Its timespan, if any, is when it was in use, not when anyone travelled it.

https://w3id.org/plato#TypeRoute

TypeSegment

A physical link with an existence of its own: a leg of a route between two stations, a reach of a river between confluences. Platforms may leave segments out of lists and searches of places.

https://w3id.org/plato#TypeSegment

Form status¶

Starter concepts for plato:form_status. The status of the name form an attestation contributes.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

Attested

A form read in the cited source. The default form status, usually omitted.

https://w3id.org/plato#Attested

Editorial

A form written by the editors of the cited edition in their own words (commentary, apparatus, notes), not a form the edited source itself contains. Use it when the citation is to an edition and the name occurs in the editors’ text, not in the edited text. It says nothing about whether the place’s name is attested elsewhere. Attested remains the default for forms the source contains.

https://w3id.org/plato#Editorial

Headword

The editorial or preferred form under which the cited authority files the entry. A scholarly decision rather than a reading.

https://w3id.org/plato#Headword

Normalised

A form derived from an attested one for matching or search, attested by no source and carrying no independent evidential value.

https://w3id.org/plato#Normalised

Preferred

The form the contributing project prefers for display and search: a project decision, so the cited source may show a different form.

https://w3id.org/plato#Preferred

Reconstructed

A form inferred but not attested; the philologists’ asterisked form.

https://w3id.org/plato#Reconstructed

Geometry role¶

Starter concepts for plato:geometry_role. What a geometry depicts, as distinct from how precisely it is known.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

Extent

The built or bounded extent of the SpatialEntity.

https://w3id.org/plato#Extent

FeaturePoint

The location of a specific central feature of the SpatialEntity, such as a market place or a church.

https://w3id.org/plato#FeaturePoint

Itinerary

A line tracing a route or journey.

https://w3id.org/plato#Itinerary

LabelAnchor

The anchor point of a map label.

https://w3id.org/plato#LabelAnchor

RepresentativePoint

A proxy point standing in for the whole SpatialEntity, for indexing and display; it need not depict any feature of it.

https://w3id.org/plato#RepresentativePoint

Meta-attestation type¶

Starter concepts for plato:has_meta_type. The nature of a meta-attestation’s relationship to its target attestation.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

AlternativeTo

This attestation and the target are alternative readings of the same evidence: at most one of them is right. Each keeps its own certainty. Link every alternative in a set to the same first one. A set of alternatives is one piece of evidence, not several, so it must be counted once.

https://w3id.org/plato#AlternativeTo

Annotates

Adds a remark about the target attestation, with its own source, without supporting, contradicting or refining it. The remark is the meta-attestation’s notes.

https://w3id.org/plato#Annotates

Bundles

Groups the target with other related attestations.

https://w3id.org/plato#Bundles

Challenges

Questions the validity of the target attestation.

https://w3id.org/plato#Challenges

Contradicts

Asserts that the target attestation is incorrect.

https://w3id.org/plato#Contradicts

DerivedFrom

This attestation’s value was derived from the target attestation’s, as a normalised search form is derived from an attested spelling.

https://w3id.org/plato#DerivedFrom

Refines

Provides more precise information than the target attestation.

https://w3id.org/plato#Refines

Retracts

The target attestation is withdrawn: it no longer holds, and is kept only so that earlier states of the gazetteer can be reproduced. For a claim its maker takes back, such as a bad import, where plato:Contradicts would read as one scholar disputing another. A tool showing the current state leaves a retracted attestation out. A retraction is itself an attestation: retracting it withdraws the withdrawal, and the target holds again.

https://w3id.org/plato#Retracts

Supersedes

Replaces the target attestation with newer information.

https://w3id.org/plato#Supersedes

Supports

Provides additional evidence for the target attestation.

https://w3id.org/plato#Supports

Occurrence context¶

Starter concepts for plato:occurrence_context. The capacity in which an attested form occurs in its source.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

Direct

The form refers to the SpatialEntity directly. The default occurrence context, usually omitted.

https://w3id.org/plato#Direct

InFieldName

The form survives only as an element of a later minor name, such as a field name.

https://w3id.org/plato#InFieldName

InPersonalName

The form occurs only as an element of a personal name, a surname or by-name; the (p) of the place-name surveys.

https://w3id.org/plato#InPersonalName

Relative qualifier¶

Starter concepts for plato:relative_qualifier. How a facet is defined relative to its anchor when bearing and distance are not applicable or not sufficient.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

BetweenXAndY

Between two anchors, given as two values of plato:relative_to, in no particular order.

https://w3id.org/plato#BetweenXAndY

Beyond

On the far side of the anchor from the point of view of the source.

https://w3id.org/plato#Beyond

DuringReignOf

A timespan defined by the reign or tenure of the anchor.

https://w3id.org/plato#DuringReignOf

Near

Close to the anchor, without a stated bearing or distance.

https://w3id.org/plato#Near

UpstreamOf

Upstream of the anchor along a watercourse.

https://w3id.org/plato#UpstreamOf

VariantOf

A name defined as a variant of the anchor name.

https://w3id.org/plato#VariantOf

Within

Inside the anchor.

https://w3id.org/plato#Within

Source stance¶

Starter concepts for plato:source_stance. How firmly the source itself asserts what an attestation records, as distinct from anyone’s confidence in it.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

StanceAsserted

The source states it as so, on its own account. The default source stance, usually omitted.

https://w3id.org/plato#StanceAsserted

StanceDoubted

The source raises it and declines to settle it. Not a denial, which is plato:negated.

https://w3id.org/plato#StanceDoubted

StanceReported

The source passes it on without vouching for it: ‘it is said’, ‘as Cambden noteth’.

https://w3id.org/plato#StanceReported

StanceTentative

The source states it, but with a hedge: ‘as it seems’, ‘were (if not are)’.

https://w3id.org/plato#StanceTentative

Timespan role¶

Starter concepts for plato:timespan_role. What an attestation’s timespans date: when what it records held, or the span of the evidence for it.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

EvidenceSpan

The span of the evidence: the dates of the earliest and latest documents that mention the entity. Not the dates of the entity itself.

https://w3id.org/plato#EvidenceSpan

WhenTrue

The period during which what the attestation records held, according to the source. The default.

https://w3id.org/plato#WhenTrue

Transcription accuracy¶

Starter concepts for plato:transcription_accuracy. How faithfully a transcribed value renders what the source shows.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

TranscriptionAccurate

The transcription renders what the source shows.

https://w3id.org/plato#TranscriptionAccurate

TranscriptionFalse

The transcribed form is not in the source at all: a ghost form, kept because it circulates and must be recognisable.

https://w3id.org/plato#TranscriptionFalse

TranscriptionInaccurate

The transcription departs from what the source shows, by misreading or error, but is a reading of something the source contains.

https://w3id.org/plato#TranscriptionInaccurate

Transcription completeness¶

Starter concepts for plato:transcription_completeness. Whether a transcribed value is whole, and if not, whether the lost part can be supplied.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

TranscriptionComplete

Nothing of the value is lost.

https://w3id.org/plato#TranscriptionComplete

TranscriptionNonReconstructable

Part of the value is lost and cannot be supplied.

https://w3id.org/plato#TranscriptionNonReconstructable

TranscriptionReconstructable

Part of the value is lost, damaged or abbreviated, but can be supplied with confidence.

https://w3id.org/plato#TranscriptionReconstructable

Certainty levels¶

The words that the certainty_level column, and certaintyLevel in JSON, can name, for certainty given in words rather than as a number.

Value

Meaning

Full identifier

Certain

The statement is certain: the source, or the contributor, has no doubt of it.

https://w3id.org/plato#Certain

LessCertain

The statement is probable but not certain.

https://w3id.org/plato#LessCertain

Uncertain

The statement is doubtful: possible, but not established.

https://w3id.org/plato#Uncertain

Relation types¶

The relations that the relation_type column of the relations sheet, and relationType in JSON, can name.

Value

Reads as

Inverse

Meaning

BeginsAt

begins_at

beginning_of

The subject segment begins at the target: a leg of an itinerary at the station it leaves, a river reach at its upstream confluence. With plato:EndsAt it gives a segment a direction. A segment with no direction is joined to its two ends with plato:HasEnd.

BirthplaceOf

birthplace_of

born_at

The subject is where the target person was born.

ConnectedTo

connected_to

connected_to

The subject and target are directly connected, in no particular direction: an attestation that A is connected to B says the same as one that B is connected to A. For a connection that runs one way, use plato:LeadsTo. A connection known in both directions from sources that each give one is better recorded as two LeadsTo attestations, each with its own source, than as one ConnectedTo.

ContainedIn

contained_in

contains

The subject SpatialEntity is contained in the target SpatialEntity, for the attested timespan: a parish in a hundred, a hundred in a county. Not an identity relation and not fixed by the class model; it is a claim, with a source and a certainty, like any other attestation. When a dataset’s containing regions are matched to a gazetteer (a parish’s county and country, looked up level by level), two claims are made, and kept apart. The dataset’s own claim is ContainedIn, pointing at a region minted from the dataset itself, one for each full chain of containers (England, Surrey), never for the name alone, so that two regions with one name in different chains are not merged. The reviewer’s decision that this region is the gazetteer’s is an IdentityRelation from it, whose promoted_from names the Candidate that holds the matching software’s similarity_score. A match score is not a certainty, so it is never put on the containment. In Linked Places Format the two are written together as one gvp:broaderPartitive relation: its relationTo is the identity’s object, its certainty the identity attestation’s certainty level, its whg_match_score the Candidate’s score and its label the region’s name. A region assigned by hand, with no software suggestion, may be stated directly against the gazetteer’s address instead.

DeathplaceOf

deathplace_of

died_at

The subject is where the target person died.

DepictedIn

depicted_in

depicts

The subject is shown in the target: a photograph, map, plan, drawing or other image, identified by its address in a collection.

EndsAt

ends_at

end_of

The subject segment ends at the target: a leg of an itinerary at the station it reaches, a river reach at its downstream confluence. See plato:BeginsAt.

FindspotOf

findspot_of

found_at

The target object (a coin, an inscription, a manuscript) was found at the subject. The timespan is when it was found, if known.

HasEnd

has_end

one_end_of

The subject segment has the target as one of its ends, in no particular direction: a stretch of road between two towns, walked either way. An undirected segment has two HasEnd attestations. Not plato:ConnectedTo, which joins two places directly: were a segment connected to its ends, every place would be two steps from its neighbours.

HomelandOf

homeland_of

has_homeland

The subject is land that the target people lived in or held, as the source says: the target is a people described elsewhere, identified by its address where it has one, and named by plato:related_label. A people is not itself a SpatialEntity.

LeadsTo

leads_to

reached_from

A directed connection, from the subject (attests_about) to the target (relates_to): one river flowing into another, a post road carrying mail from one town to the next, letters sent from one city to another. It joins two places; the reaches and legs of a network are segments, whose direction is given by plato:BeginsAt and plato:EndsAt. Not river-specific: a project declares its own narrower types (‘flows into’, ‘carries post to’) with plato:broader_relation pointing here, so that a consumer that knows only LeadsTo still follows them in the right direction.

MemberOf

member_of

has_member

The subject is a member of the target route, itinerary or network: a station on a road, a stop on a journey, a port in a trade network, a segment of a river system. Its position, where the source gives one, is plato:sequence on the same attestation. A route may be a member of another route (one stage of a pilgrimage), but not, through any chain of memberships, of itself. Not containment: a town on a road is not within it, and a network’s members need not lie inside anything.

ResidenceOf

residence_of

resided_at

The target person or group lived at the subject, for the attested timespan.

SettingOf

setting_of

took_place_at

The target event (a battle, a council, a fair) took place at the subject, wholly or in part.

SubjectOf

subject_of

about

The target is about the subject: an archival file, a site record, a report or a publication, identified by its address. Not a citation of the target as evidence for another statement, which is plato:has_citation.

WorkplaceOf

workplace_of

worked_at

The target person or group worked, or held office, at the subject, for the attested timespan: Linked Traces’ heldOffice, and work generally.
