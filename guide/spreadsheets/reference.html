Sheet reference¶

Where this page fits

This is the column-by-column reference for temPlato, PLATO’s spreadsheet template. If you are new to it, start with Organising data in spreadsheets, which explains the ten sheets and the rules that apply to all of them, and where to download the template.

Spreadsheets are one of several formats that comply with PLATO. The same data can be written as PLATO JSON or as linked data (RDF), and PLATO tools converts between them, and to and from Linked Places Format.

Every column of every sheet: whether it is needed, what to put in it, an example, and the values it accepts. This page is generated from the table definitions, so it always matches them.

In every sheet except about, places, sources and identities, each row becomes one PLATO attestation, and these columns are shared: place_id, date, from, to, source_id, locator, attribution, citation_function, certainty, certainty_level, denied, stance and notes.

about¶

One row describing the dataset as a whole: its title, who made it, its licence, what it covers, and the base of its web addresses. It is what makes the dataset findable and citable (the FAIR principles). Exactly one row. Only the title is required, and the licence too once status is ‘published’. The creator_name column is deprecated: names go in creator, which takes a name, a web address, or a name with its address.

places¶

One row per place, or other spatial entity, that your data is about: a settlement, a region, a river, a road, an administrative unit. Each row becomes a plato:SpatialEntity. Everything else about a place (its names, locations, types) goes in the other sheets, where it can carry a date and a source.

relations¶

One row for each time a source relates a place to something else: a parish lying in a hundred, a station on a road, a stop on a journey, one place leading to another, a place where a person was born. Each row becomes a plato:Attestation relating the place in place_id either to another place, in related_place_id, or to something described elsewhere (a person, an object, an event), in related_uri. Fill in one of the two, never both; or neither, when the source only names the other thing, in related_label. CSVW cannot state that rule, so PLATO tools checks it.

Column

Needed?

What to put

Example

Values

place_id

required

The place this row is about: a place_id from the places sheet.

bristol

relation_type

required

How they are related. Places within places: ‘ContainedIn’ (the place in place_id lies within the other). Routes, journeys and networks: ‘MemberOf’ (the place is a station, stop or member of the route, journey or network in related_place_id; give its position in sequence), ‘ConnectedTo’ (directly connected, either way), ‘LeadsTo’ (connected one way, from place_id to the other), ‘BeginsAt’ and ‘EndsAt’ (a segment, such as a leg of a road, begins or ends at the other place), ‘HasEnd’ (the other place is one end of a segment with no direction; give it for both ends). People, objects and events, in related_uri: ‘BirthplaceOf’, ‘DeathplaceOf’, ‘ResidenceOf’, ‘FindspotOf’, ‘SettingOf’, ‘WorkplaceOf’. Images and records about a place, in related_uri: ‘DepictedIn’ (a photograph, map or drawing shows it), ‘SubjectOf’ (an archival file, report or publication is about it). A people’s land: ‘HomelandOf’ (the place is land the people lived in or held; name the people in related_label, and give its address in related_uri where it has one). Only PLATO’s own relation types can be used here; a project’s own (declared in JSON or RDF with broader_relation) cannot, and further types are added to PLATO’s vocabulary on request.

connections¶

One row for each figure a source gives about a connection between two places: the number of letters sent from one city to another, a journey time, a toll, a cargo. Each row becomes a plato:Attestation relating the place in place_id to the place in related_place_id, with one plato:PropertyValue. Several figures about one connection are several rows. A connection with no figures goes in the relations sheet; a physical link with its own existence, such as a leg of a road, is better entered as a place of its own, with its figures in the properties sheet.
