PLATO — Place Attestation Ontology: Description back to ToC

An ontology for representing historical place knowledge as attestations: bundles of evidence linking SpatialEntities (settlements, routes, networks, administrative units, regions and other entities whose identity is bound up with space) to Names, Geometries, Timespans, Types, PropertyValues and Authorities with full provenance. Developed by the Pelagios Network's Place Working Group, led by the Institute for Spatial History Innovation (ISHI), University of Pittsburgh. PLATO formalises the WHG v4 data model, in which the Attestation is the fundamental unit of contributed knowledge. The Linked Places Format (LPF) is defined as its single-object-attestation profile: every LPF element corresponds to one attestation linking one entity to one name, type, geometry or related entity, so existing LPF files remain valid. The use cases of Linked Traces are accommodated within the same model.

Candidatec back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#Candidate

A match between two SpatialEntities suggested by software, for a person to review: reconciliation against reference gazetteers (as in WHG's Map your Data), record linkage, clustering. A Candidate carries a similarity score, the algorithm's version, optionally the settings that produced the score (plato:match_parameters), when it was generated, and the status it had when its CandidateSet was issued, 'suggested'. It is a claim by no one, and never becomes an IdentityRelation: a reviewer's answer is an Attestation bundling an IdentityRelation that points back through plato:promoted_from, a match or, with plato:negated, a denial. A Candidate is published only in a CandidateSet, and is frozen from then on, like a published attestation: never changed, never deleted, so that a promoted_from pointing at it keeps its meaning. What became of it is therefore derived, not stored. Take the attestations as of a moment (those created by then, less those retracted or superseded; see plato:Gazetteer) whose identity relations point at the Candidate: it is accepted where some are and none is negated, rejected where some are negated and none is not, answered both ways where there are both, and unanswered where there are none. Identity is attributed, so a consumer may ask this of one reviewer's or one gazetteer's attestations only. Its IRI is a fragment of the IRI of the CandidateSet that publishes it, minted from what it says: the set's IRI without any fragment, then '#c-' and the first 8 hex digits (lower case) of the SHA-256 of a JSON array of four strings, the source's IRI, the candidate's IRI, the algorithm version and the match parameters (empty when there are none), written in the JSON Canonicalization Scheme (RFC 8785) and hashed as UTF-8 with no Unicode normalisation. Where 8 digits would begin like another Candidate's hash, in its set or in an earlier set given to the minter, it takes the shortest prefix, in steps of 4 digits, that differs from every other. An IRI once published is never changed. A later set leaves out a Candidate an earlier set already published, so each Candidate has one IRI, under the set that first published it. The time it was generated is not hashed. '#a-' is the form of an attestation's IRI, never a Candidate's.

Candidate Setc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#CandidateSet

The matches one run of matching software suggested for a dataset's SpatialEntities (plato:candidates_for), published together for people to review and for the dataset's identity relations to point at (plato:promoted_from). A Candidate is published only in a candidate set, never in a Gazetteer: a Gazetteer holds claims, and a Candidate is a claim by no one. A candidate set is issued once (dcterms:issued) and frozen from then on: no Candidate in it is changed or deleted, and a later run is a new set. A dataset whose identity relations cite a candidate set should have that set published wherever the dataset is, so that promoted_from can be followed; a validator that has the set should warn when a promoted_from names no Candidate in it. As a dcat:Dataset it is titled with dcterms:title and licensed with dcterms:license, as a Gazetteer is. It is disjoint from Gazetteer, so it carries none of a Gazetteer's own properties (a status, an owner): its date of issue says from when it is frozen.

Gazetteerc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#Gazetteer

A mutable, versioned workspace of SpatialEntities and Attestations, owned by a contributor or team. Replaces the WHG v3 concepts of Dataset, Dataset Collection, and Place Collection. A Gazetteer can be incrementally edited, enriched, and corrected without re-ingestion. A Gazetteer's attestations need not be about SpatialEntities it defines. It may attest evidence about SpatialEntities defined in other Gazetteers, identified by their IRIs, without redefining them: a correction to another project's place, a link from a record to an existing place, a curator's selection of places for a collection. Such an attestation adds evidence. It does not change the entity's identity or make this Gazetteer its owner, and its source, contributor and status are this Gazetteer's. The append-only rule of a published Gazetteer applies to the attestations it contains, not to everything said elsewhere about its entities. In PLATO JSON such attestations go in an attestation-centric document, whose 'about' gives the entity's IRI; a place-centric document declares every SpatialEntity it lists (plato:contains_entity), so it lists only its own. As a dcat:Dataset, a Gazetteer is titled with dcterms:title and licensed with dcterms:license. Not with plato:authority_title or plato:licence: their domains are plato:Authority and plato:Dataset, so using them would make the Gazetteer one of the Authorities it contains attestations about. Described for discovery and citation (the FAIR principles), again in the vocabularies a catalogue already reads: dcterms:creator for the people to cite (plato:gazetteer_owner, its owner, is not necessarily an author); dcat:keyword; dcterms:spatial for the area it covers, as an address; dcterms:temporal for the years it covers, a dcterms:PeriodOfTime with dcat:startDate and dcat:endDate; dcat:landingPage; and void:uriSpace, the base from which its entities' IRIs are made. A published Gazetteer (plato:gazetteer_status 'published') must state its dcterms:license: data others are to reuse needs its terms stated. All the other descriptive properties are optional. Versions, and so citations that reproduce. A SpatialEntity's evidence grows and is corrected while its identity stays the same, so a citation of it must say which state was meant. For the same reason as above, a Gazetteer's version uses DCAT 3's own properties, not a PLATO one: dcat:version for its version indicator, and, for a frozen snapshot published under its own IRI, dcat:isVersionOf (the living Gazetteer) and dcat:previousVersion (the snapshot before). A SpatialEntity is then cited as its IRI plus the Gazetteer version, one number per gazetteer rather than one per entity, as PeriodO cites. Once a Gazetteer's plato:gazetteer_status is 'published', its attestations are append-only, and this is normative: an attestation is never deleted or changed. What an attestation says includes its facets and citations (plato:attests_name, attests_geometry, attests_timespan, attests_type, attests_property, attests_identity, plato:has_citation) whether or not they have IRIs of their own: a Name shared under its IRI is not re-spelt once published, since that would change every attestation pointing to it. The SpatialEntities and Authorities that attestations are about or cite are not attestations, and their descriptions may still be corrected and enriched. Every attestation in a published Gazetteer should have a permanent IRI of its own, since a correction or withdrawal must point to its target; this is a recommendation, meant to become a requirement. A correction is a new attestation with a meta-attestation (plato:Supersedes, plato:Contradicts), and a withdrawal is a meta-attestation of type plato:Retracts. Each carries plato:created. The state as of any moment D is then a filter over the data: the attestations created by D, less those superseded or retracted by D. A supersession or retraction takes effect only while it holds itself, so retracting a retraction restores its target, and a chain of them resolves the same way; a cycle is an error in the data. A platform can offer that state on request, by datetime negotiation on the entity's IRI (RFC 7089, Memento); PLATO recommends this but does not require it, since it is behaviour of a server, not of data.

Geometryc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#Geometry

A spatial representation of a SpatialEntity's extent or location. Geometries are independent, reusable entities. The same Geometry may be referenced by multiple Attestations across different SpatialEntities or time periods. Each Geometry is one GeoJSON geometry; a GeometryCollection is not accepted, so a set of different kinds (a point and a polygon) is several Geometry nodes. One attestation may attest several geometries when one source gives them all, each with a plato:geometry_role where they depict different things (a built extent, a label position); a geometry from another source is another attestation, since it has another provenance. See examples/geometry-roles.ttl. A geometry traced from a historical map, and placed in the world through a georeference (a IIIF Georeference Annotation, such as one made in Allmaps), is evidence of what the map shows, so it is attested, not plato:computed. Its position depends on the map, on the annotation, whose control points can be edited after the tracing, and on the transformation fitted to them. So the attestation cites both, and no new property is needed: the map as the evidence (plato:citation_function cito:citesAsEvidence), with the canvas as the plato:locator, and '#xywh=' added when the tracing is from a region of it; and the annotation as a Source of its own, with the annotation's IRI, plato:derived_from the map's Source, cited with cito:usesMethodIn. The transformation (polynomial of order 1 to 3, thin plate spline, projective, Helmert) and the number of control points used go in the attestation's plato:notes, since the annotation may since have changed. A position that is where the map writes a name takes plato:geometry_role plato:LabelAnchor. The position of a map symbol is a plato:RepresentativePoint, unless the map shows the specific feature (a church drawn as such), when it is a plato:FeaturePoint. See schemas/examples/place-centric-georeference.json.

Identity Relationc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#IdentityRelation

An assertion that two SpatialEntities refer to the same real-world entity. Carries its own provenance (who asserted it, on what basis), certainty (how confident the assertion is), and identity semantics (exactMatch, closeMatch, related). Identity relations are distinct from scholarly entity-to-entity relations, which describe substantive historical relationships between different entities. They are also distinct from Candidates, which are matches suggested by software and are claims by no one; a relation made in answer to one points to it (plato:promoted_from). An IdentityRelation is one attributed claim among many, never a canonical statement of fact. A platform must not materialise identity relations into merged records or minted identifiers, nor let one outweigh contrary evidence by fiat: it is weighed like any other evidence, and someone else may say the opposite (an attestation with plato:negated bundling an exactMatch says two entities are not the same). Identity relations asserted together, as when a person accepts a cluster of matches, are bundled by one Attestation (plato:attests_identity), which carries their shared provenance and is withdrawn or amended as a unit. A consumer may take the transitive closure of exactMatch relations within one such attestation, and must not chain identity relations across different attestations, nor chain closeMatch, related or unspecified ones at all: A the same as B and B the same as C, asserted by different people, is not anyone's assertion that A is C.

Property Valuec back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#PropertyValue

A quantitative or descriptive attribute of a SpatialEntity, expressed as a property drawn from an external vocabulary together with its value. Covers the many things a historical source says that are neither a name, a location, a period nor a classification — populations, valuations, elevations, recurrence rules for markets and fairs. The property itself is identified by URI rather than enumerated here, so the vocabulary stays open and delegated (Wikidata properties, CIDOC-CRM, project vocabularies), in the same way that Type delegates its classifications to AAT. Values may be literals (with a datatype and optional unit) or structured objects whose shape is declared by plato:value_type. As a facet node, a PropertyValue may carry the qualification properties (certainty, fuzziness) like any other. Where the value is another SpatialEntity, use an entity-to-entity relation instead; this class is for attributes of the entity. A person or organisation connected to it (the grantee of a market right, an owner, a lord of the manor) is such an attribute: the value is the agent's IRI, ideally in an authority such as Wikidata or VIAF, with value_datatype xsd:anyURI, and the property names the role, for example http://www.wikidata.org/prop/direct/P127 ('owned by'). This asserts nothing false, and needs no agent class: PLATO's own agent classes (Contributor, Organization) describe who recorded the data. A figure from a statistical table (a census count, a toll receipt) is a PropertyValue that is also a qb:Observation of the W3C RDF Data Cube vocabulary. It carries qb:dataSet (the table, a qb:DataSet), one direct statement for each of its coordinates with the table's qb:DimensionProperty predicates, one for each fact about the figure with qb:AttributeProperty predicates (sdmx-attribute:obsStatus for a cell's state), and plato:universe for the figure it is a part or share of. So the county is not said to have 'class 1': the observation is. The measure stays plato:property_type and plato:value_literal, and the area and date stay on the attestation; a cube export adds the direct measure statement, sdmx-dimension:refArea and refPeriod. A printed dash is an observation with an obsStatus and no value, never 0. Money is an exact integer in the smallest unit of its system of account (farthings, for sterling amounts with halfpennies), the unit an IRI from a fitting authority or minted by the encoding project, ideally naming a unit of account in a named system ('penny sterling'); PLATO mints no currency and converts nothing. A coordinate the source does not give is an explicit 'unknown' code. A table mixing measures is one qb:DataSet per measure. Dimension values are concepts, with the source's label as skos:prefLabel, in code lists the encoding project publishes where none exists. A statistical table in spreadsheet form keeps its own CSV on the Web description and links to places by place_id. The design, and its testing on three corpora, is issue #14. A latitude given without a longitude (a source's 'at 9.5 degrees north') is a PropertyValue, not a Geometry, since it is not a location: property http://www.w3.org/2003/01/geo/wgs84_pos#lat, the value in decimal degrees. Spatial Entityc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#SpatialEntity

An entity whose identity is bound up with space, whether its spatial extent is attested directly, applies notionally (as with the region to which a period applies), or is derived from its constituents (as with a route or a network, whose extent is that of its members). A SpatialEntity need not have any attested geometry: lost, imagined and unlocated entities are admissible. A curated selection of other places, such as a thematic collection, is not a SpatialEntity: it is a Gazetteer, whose attestations are about the places it selects. Many SpatialEntities are what people would call places and some are not, and the word is used differently across languages and disciplines. The ontology therefore neither equates a SpatialEntity with a place nor defines a place in terms of SpatialEntities or their attestations. Contained Inni back to ToC or Named Individual ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#ContainedIn

The subject SpatialEntity is contained in the target SpatialEntity, for the attested timespan: a parish in a hundred, a hundred in a county. Not an identity relation and not fixed by the class model; it is a claim, with a source and a certainty, like any other attestation. When a dataset's containing regions are matched to a gazetteer (a parish's county and country, looked up level by level), two claims are made, and kept apart. The dataset's own claim is ContainedIn, pointing at a region minted from the dataset itself, one for each full chain of containers (England, Surrey), never for the name alone, so that two regions with one name in different chains are not merged. The reviewer's decision that this region is the gazetteer's is an IdentityRelation from it, whose promoted_from names the Candidate that holds the matching software's similarity_score. A match score is not a certainty, so it is never put on the containment. In Linked Places Format the two are written together as one gvp:broaderPartitive relation: its relationTo is the identity's object, its certainty the identity attestation's certainty level, its whg_match_score the Candidate's score and its label the region's name. A region assigned by hand, with no software suggestion, may be stated directly against the gazetteer's address instead.
