PLATO — Place Attestation Ontology: Description back to ToC

An ontology for representing historical place knowledge as attestations: bundles of evidence linking SpatialEntities (settlements, routes, networks, administrative units, regions and other entities whose identity is bound up with space) to Names, Geometries, Timespans, Types, PropertyValues and Authorities with full provenance. Developed by the Pelagios Network's Place Working Group, led by the Institute for Spatial History Innovation (ISHI), University of Pittsburgh. PLATO formalises the WHG v4 data model, in which the Attestation is the fundamental unit of contributed knowledge. The Linked Places Format (LPF) is defined as its single-object-attestation profile: every LPF element corresponds to one attestation linking one entity to one name, type, geometry or related entity, so existing LPF files remain valid. The use cases of Linked Traces are accommodated within the same model.

Attestationc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#Attestation

A bundle of evidence asserting that a particular SpatialEntity had a particular Name, Geometry, Type, or combination thereof, during a particular Timespan, according to a particular Authority. The Attestation node carries only metadata; its semantic content is defined entirely by its outgoing relationships. IMPORTANT: Attestations are document nodes in the graph, not edges. They are instances of plato:Attestation (a class), not instances of owl:ObjectProperty. This design enables meta-attestations and full provenance tracking.

Citationc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#Citation

One attestation's use of one Authority: the qualified form of plato:sourced_by. Carries what is true of this citation rather than of the source or of the attestation: the locator within the work (page, folio, column, table cell, entry), and whether the attribution was stated in the source or inferred editorially. Identity Relationc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#IdentityRelation

An assertion that two SpatialEntities refer to the same real-world entity. Property Valuec back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#PropertyValue

A quantitative or descriptive attribute of a SpatialEntity, expressed as a property drawn from an external vocabulary together with its value. Covers the many things a historical source says that are neither a name, a location, a period nor a classification — populations, valuations, elevations, recurrence rules for markets and fairs. The property itself is identified by URI rather than enumerated here, so the vocabulary stays open and delegated (Wikidata properties, CIDOC-CRM, project vocabularies), in the same way that Type delegates its classifications to AAT. Values may be literals (with a datatype and optional unit) or structured objects whose shape is declared by plato:value_type. As a facet node, a PropertyValue may carry the qualification properties (certainty, fuzziness) like any other. Where the value is itself an entity, use a entity-to-entity relation instead; this class is for attributes, not relationships.

Spatial Entityc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#SpatialEntity

An entity whose identity is bound up with space, whether its spatial extent is attested directly, applies notionally (as with the region to which a period applies), or is derived from its constituents (as with a collection). A SpatialEntity need not have any attested geometry: lost, imagined and unlocated entities are admissible. What kind of entity it is (settlement, route, region, administrative unit, period and so on) is not fixed by the class. It is established by Type attestations, so a classification is a claim rather than an axiom. SpatialEntities are not defined by any single attestation. They are the point of convergence for many attestations from different sources, periods and contributors. A SpatialEntity is not a place, and PLATO defines no Place class. "Place" carries several meanings in the gazetteer community, and which of them, if any, corresponds to a SpatialEntity is contested; the ontology does not settle it. Neither a SpatialEntity nor any set of attestations or of SpatialEntities is a place by virtue of this ontology. Where the word appears in this document it is used informally, for whatever a source is talking about.
has super-classes
Entity c
is in domain of
country codes dp, identifier dp, namespace dp
is in range of

attribution statusop back to ToC or Object Property ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#attribution_status

Whether the attribution of the evidence to the cited source was stated by the source or resolved editorially. Should point to a concept in a SKOS concept scheme. form statusop back to ToC or Object Property ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#form_status

The status of the name form this attestation contributes: read in the cited source, chosen by the cited authority, or derived by the contributing project. Should point to a concept in a SKOS concept scheme. The starter concepts are: - plato:Attested: a form read in the cited source. The default, and usually omitted. - plato:Headword: the editorial or preferred form under which the cited authority files the entry ('Bunsty Hundred'). A scholarly decision rather than a reading, and typically the form users search for. Another authority may choose differently, which is why it is a sourced attestation and not a label on the SpatialEntity. - plato:Normalised: a form derived from an attested one for matching or search ('Bunstow(e)' resolved to 'Bunstowe'), attested by no source and carrying no independent evidential value. It may point to the attestation it was derived from through a meta-attestation. - plato:Reconstructed: a form inferred but not attested, the philologists' asterisked form. A different axis from plato:name_type, which classifies what the name denotes: a headword is still a toponym. It sits on the Attestation, not on the Name, because the same string can be a reading in one attestation and a headword in another. The WHG ingestion prototype reached the same placement independently: its preferred-name flag is on the attestation, not on the toponym record.
has domain
Attestation c
has range
Concept c

geometry roleop back to ToC or Object Property ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/plato#geometry_role

What this geometry depicts, as distinct from how precisely it is known (plato:spatial_precision) or where its indexing point falls (plato:repr_point). A SpatialEntity may be attested with several geometries at once — a built extent, the location of a specific central feature, a proxy standing in for the whole, the anchor of a map label — and without a declared role a consumer cannot tell them apart, nor state which one an analysis used. Should point to a concept in a SKOS concept scheme (e.g. plato:Extent, plato:FeaturePoint, plato:RepresentativePoint, plato:LabelAnchor, plato:Itinerary). Using URIs rather than free text lets gazetteers that record geometry differently be compared. Note that role is orthogonal to precision: a proxy locator may be surveyed to the metre and still not depict the SpatialEntity it stands for. A parish church's coordinates contributed as a locator for a town are a representative point of high precision, not an imprecise feature point.
